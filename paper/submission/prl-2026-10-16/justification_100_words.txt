We identify a resource law for memory in noisy quantum dynamics. At fixed accuracy and independent depolarizing noise, the greatest attainable memory depth grows exponentially with the number of qubits reinitialized per step, independently of reservoir size after initialization decays. A uniform upper bound and an explicit construction establish both sides of this scaling. Additional unital noise preserves the bound, while cooling supplies another entropy sink. This connects quantum information retention, open system dynamics, and physical computing through a shared resource accounting. We believe this perspective can interest physicists beyond reservoir computing and guide the design of driven quantum devices.
