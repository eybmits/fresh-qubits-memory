Dear Editors,

We submit the manuscript "The Fundamental Memory Ceiling of Noisy Quantum Reservoirs" for consideration as a Letter in Physical Review Letters.

Quantum reservoir computing processes time series with the transient dynamics of noisy quantum hardware, and for temporal tasks its decisive resource is memory. The natural expectation is that a larger reservoir remembers longer. The Letter proves that adding qubits cannot raise the uniform memory ceiling: with k input qubits reinitialized per time step, the longest memory that any reservoir can attain under depolarizing noise is exp[Theta(k)] time steps, whatever its size, dynamics, and input encoding. The upper bound holds for every member of a well-defined class of noisy quantum dynamics, and an explicit error-correcting reservoir attains its exponential scaling. Because the proof uses only the entropy that a time step can remove, the result extends to general noise through an entropy budget and applies to any noisy quantum system supplied with fresh qubits, including measurement-free error correction.

The result is of broad interest. It identifies entropy removal, not size, as the resource that sets memory, for every architecture within the noise model, and it gives a concrete design target for quantum reservoir computing on current hardware: standard Ising and Haar-random reservoirs operate far below the ceiling, whereas a reservoir that uses its fresh qubits for repair approaches it. With finite readout precision, the bound also limits the memory capacity, the memory measure most widely used in the field.

The Supplemental Material contains the complete proofs, the exact analysis of the error-correcting reservoir, the entropy budget for general noise, and the details of the simulations; the accompanying archive contains all code and data. The use of AI-based tools is disclosed in the Acknowledgments and in the Supplemental Material.

The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere.
