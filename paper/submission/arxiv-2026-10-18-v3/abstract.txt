Adding qubits seems the natural way to extend the memory of a noisy quantum reservoir. We show that it is not: with $k$ input qubits reinitialized per time step, the longest memory any reservoir can attain under depolarizing noise is $\exp[\Theta(k)]$ time steps, whatever its size, dynamics, and input encoding. A universal upper bound and an explicit reservoir that attains the exponential scaling establish this optimal resource law. It extends to an entropy budget for general noise and applies to any noisy quantum dynamics refreshed by fresh qubits, including measurement-free error correction.
