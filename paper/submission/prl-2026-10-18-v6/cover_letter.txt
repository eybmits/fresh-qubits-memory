To the Editors of Physical Review Letters

Dear Editors,

Please consider “The Fundamental Memory Ceiling of Noisy Quantum Reservoirs,” by Markus Baumann, Jonas Stein, and Claudia Linnhoff-Popien, for publication as a Letter in Physical Review Letters.

Main result

How much memory can fresh qubits sustain under noise? Under local Markovian depolarizing noise, we prove that the optimal input-memory depth scales as exp[Θ(k)] with k qubits refreshed per step, independently of total system size. This holds after initialization washout, at fixed noise strength and a fixed, sufficiently small distinguishability threshold. The upper bound covers arbitrary reservoir unitaries and input encodings within the model, without external classical memory or feedback. A unitary, measurement-free error-correcting construction attains the exponential scaling. Retaining an input for T steps therefore requires Θ(log T) refreshed qubits per step, and our construction attains this scaling.

Why Physical Review Letters

Entropy removal sets the memory ceiling. Because the bound depends on an entropy-removal budget, its scope extends beyond reservoir computing to other quantum dynamics under the same noise and resource assumptions. This connects quantum information, open-system dynamics, and physical computing through an optimal resource law. Suitable error-correcting reservoirs with mid-circuit resets provide a route to testing the predicted scaling.
