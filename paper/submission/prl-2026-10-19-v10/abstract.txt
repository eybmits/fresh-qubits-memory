Fresh qubits, not size, set how long a noisy quantum system can remember. With k fresh qubits per time step, the longest memory under depolarizing noise is exp[Θ(k)] time steps, whatever the size and unitary: keeping an input for T steps needs Θ(log T) fresh qubits per step, and that many suffice. A universal upper bound and an explicit reservoir that attains the scaling prove this for quantum reservoirs. The bound uses only the entropy budget of a time step and holds for any Markovian noisy dynamics with fresh qubits and no classical feedback, including measurement-free error correction.
